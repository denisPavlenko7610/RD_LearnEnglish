# A1–C1 course and Android delivery plan

19 November 2026 · Planned work. [GDD](GDD.md) defines product rules. [Roadmap](ROADMAP.md) records what is already
implemented, and [Validation](VALIDATION.md) records what has actually been checked.

## Release outcome

An adult Russian-speaking learner can start from the beginning or take an optional placement check, follow lessons
through C1, and get a clear answer to three questions. What can I do independently? What should I practise next?
What can I still do when tested later in a different situation? Progress and rehearsal recordings stay local.
Listening and pronunciation may require an internet connection. Android phone landscape is the primary layout.

Course completion records which material the learner covered. Proficiency evidence records performance by skill on
unseen and delayed tasks. An external assessment can provide independent confirmation. A fixed learning time or
guaranteed C1 score cannot be inferred from either course completion or app usage. Cambridge English estimates about
700–800 **guided learning hours** from beginner to C1. Treat this as a planning reference, with app practice hours
tracked
separately. [Cambridge English guided learning hours](https://support.cambridgeenglish.org/hc/en-gb/articles/202838506-Guided-learning-hours)

## Learning sequence

1. The optional placement check samples reading, listening and short production. Recommend a starting point by
   skill and offer missing prerequisites. Permit manual selection at any level.
2. Start each unit with a practical goal and a short example. Introduce grammar, pronunciation and vocabulary
   in a dialogue, recording or text. Make feedback specific enough to explain how to improve the next response.
3. Progress from recognition to recall and then to a realistic task. Use typing, sentence assembly, listening,
   oral response and writing where each helps the learner do the task. Keep the lesson available in short sessions
   and let longer speaking and writing tasks span sessions.
4. Prioritize due reviews and recent mistakes alongside new material. Repeat missed targets in another context
   after intervening practice. Allow early review and unlimited hints. Record assisted work, independent responses
   and delayed recall separately.
5. Check the same skill later with new prompts, speakers or scenarios. If a skill is weak, recommend the specific
   prerequisite or lesson and offer a fresh check after further practice.

## Curriculum coverage

Track the selected CEFR descriptors for reception, production, interaction and mediation separately. Each selected
descriptor needs a teaching unit, an independent transfer task and an assessment criterion. The initial unit counts
are estimates for planning the authoring workload. Mark an uncovered descriptor as pending until evidence exists.
[Council of Europe CEFR Companion Volume](https://www.coe.int/en/web/education/-/common-european-framework-of-reference-for-languages-learning-teaching-assessment-14)

| Level | Planned units | Example of an independent end-of-level task                                                                                                                   |
|-------|--------------:|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| A1    |            24 | Use a sign or short recording to find the right bus, ask the driver a simple question and write a brief confirmation.                                         |
| A2    |            28 | Understand a changed booking, clarify the new details in conversation and send a short message.                                                               |
| B1    |            32 | Explain a familiar problem aloud, ask a follow-up question and write a connected account with causes and next steps.                                          |
| B2    |            36 | Compare two sources, take and defend a position in a discussion, and write a structured recommendation.                                                       |
| C1    |            24 | Synthesize a longer recording and a substantial text for a specific audience, respond to an unexpected objection, and write a nuanced, well-structured brief. |

Each unit has a stable goal ID, prerequisites used for recommendations, lexical targets and feedback for common
errors. Audit the existing pilot topics against the matrix and reuse the ones that meet the descriptor and task
requirements. Test reading for gist, detail and inference. Use recorded everyday speech at introductory levels
and add varied voices, accents, pace and implied meaning as the learner progresses.

### Vocabulary and grammar

Plan for approximately **4,500 productive senses and expressions** by the end of C1, within a broader inventory of
about **9,000 reviewed recognition entries**. These are authoring targets. Count a word meaning, collocation, idiom
or phrasal verb sense once. Inflections, duplicate cards and spelling variants do not add to these totals. Estimate
500 new productive targets at A1, 700 at A2, 1,000 at B1, 1,100 at B2 and 1,200 at C1. Adjust the allocation
after auditing frequency, task coverage and learner results.

Use the current CEFR-J and Octanove catalog as the starting inventory. Rank general English with NGSL and choose
additional targets from the language needed for the course tasks. Keep rare or specialist entries in the reference
catalog when they are useful for lookup. A 100,000-entry dictionary is unnecessary for this route. Nation's
estimates concern coverage of particular texts and count word families, a different unit from the course targets.
[Nation, vocabulary size for reading and listening](https://www.wgtn.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0018/1626120/2006-How-large-a-vocab.pdf)

- Give each taught meaning of a word, collocation, idiom or phrasal verb a stable sense ID. Store its gloss, level,
  usage pattern, register, examples and links to exercises. Explain separability, object position and common
  confusions where applicable. Group evidence from different cards that check the same sense.
- Review the 8,693 imported reference entries level by level. Add verified Russian glosses and practice
  links for teachable senses. Entries without an approved meaning or learning use stay searchable with a visible
  reference status. Track reviewed, linked and pending counts so gaps are visible. Begin with the frequent senses
  needed by the curriculum, then expand the catalogue in batches.
- Cover vocabulary in reading, listening and production across units. Include a first independent recall and a
  later application in a new context for active vocabulary. Give other recorded senses their own evidence, even
  when they share the same spelling. Apply the same review logic to expressions and phrasal verbs.
- Teach grammar through a communicative use, then revisit it in different topics. Author valid answer variants
  for short responses. Evaluate longer text and speech with rubrics and examples at different performance levels.

### Speaking, listening and writing

Use online playback for short pilot examples and the A1 bus message. Choose a supported or licensed source for
release and preserve a stable transcript, pace and speaker for reviewed listening assessments.

- Provide licensed recordings, replay and transcripts with clear controls. Keep transcripts available for
  instruction and record their use as assistance in listening checks where independent listening is being measured.
- Include recording and playback, guided spoken replies, clarification and follow-up within lessons. Offer
  local recording deletion. A declined microphone permission leaves other activities available and the speaking
  evidence pending. Recorded monologues and rehearsed dialogues are useful practice. The interaction checkpoint
  also needs an unfamiliar partner response and a follow-up the learner has to handle.
- Use rubrics for task achievement, comprehension, interaction, clarity, range and control, adapted to the
  activity. For writing, show examples and a revision pass. For oral or written work, identify whether feedback
  came from self-review, a qualified reviewer or a validated automated method. Keep automatic transcription
  separate from pronunciation or proficiency assessment.

### First implementation batch

The pilot already enables both landscape rotations and disables portrait in Unity project settings. Boot and App
are enabled in the build scene list. Device behavior is pending verification.

1. Build and install the current pilot on a physical Android phone. Exercise Boot, lessons, keyboard input,
   profile persistence, online playback and both landscape rotations. Record results and failure cases in
   [Validation](VALIDATION.md).
2. Fix layout and lifecycle issues found on the phone. Handle safe area, system bars, software keyboard,
   background/resume and loss of audio focus. Keep reading, answering and checking reachable when height shrinks.
3. Extend the existing A1 bus topic with an original recording and transcript, a spoken reply with record/playback,
   a short message, and held-out transfer material. Use this unit as the authoring reference for the rest of A1.
4. Define the descriptor/skill and vocabulary-sense data on this unit first. Build checks for missing links,
   duplicate IDs and invalid answer variants. Record baseline results before expanding authoring to other levels.

## Acceptance and evidence

### Course

- Trace every selected descriptor through instruction, practice, independent assessment and feedback. Keep
  checkpoint prompts separate from the teaching examples. Publish coverage gaps in the course checklist.
- Record baseline, checkpoint and delayed performance separately. Compare unseen tasks of similar difficulty
  after roughly one and four weeks during trials, along with the time spent studying and the hardest steps.
  Use the results to tune pacing and review. Keep individual pilot results separate from any general claim.
- Report reading, listening, speaking, writing, interaction and mediation separately. For the C1 capstone, use
  a reviewed rubric and independent human scoring for open responses unless an automated method has been
  validated against qualified raters. Report a missing skill as unassessed and recommend targeted practice.
- Before claiming established or certified C1, require independent proficiency evidence appropriate to that
  claim. Make learner-owned exports available for sharing recordings, writing and results with a reviewer.

### Android

- Test a small phone, a representative mid-range phone and a tablet or large-screen environment, including both
  intended phone rotations, display cutouts, text scaling, narrow windows, touch, Android Back and the keyboard.
  Keep a responsive fallback for Android-managed orientation and resizing on large screens.
  [Android orientation and window guidance](https://developer.android.com/develop/adaptive-apps/guides/app-orientation-aspect-ratio-resizability)
- Verify installation, launch, listening over a network, playback failure and retry, local recording, denied microphone
  access, audio interruption,
  rotation during an answer, resume after process death, low storage, failed save retry and upgrade without lost
  progress. Measure startup, memory, frame time and audio/package size on the device baseline.
- Produce a device-test APK and a signed release build. If Google Play distribution is chosen, produce the AAB
  and verify the publication requirements at release time. Include licenses and a local backup/restore path.
- Run Unity compilation, content checks, EditMode and PlayMode checks, and Android device smoke tests. Preserve
  existing content IDs and validate saved evidence across upgrades. Record actual results in
  [Validation](VALIDATION.md).
